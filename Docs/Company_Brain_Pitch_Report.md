# Company Brain: pitch readiness report

This report reads Company Brain through the L7 webinar "Pitch like a pro". The webinar makes one point again and again. An investor looks at a deck for about two and a half minutes, so every slide must answer one question with clear numbers. Each section below takes one of those questions. It gives our answer, and then it names what is still weak. Read it once before you touch the deck.

## 1. The one line

Everything starts from one sentence. The webinar says to write it first, then turn each slide's sentence into its headline.

> Company Brain gives Sri Lankan SMEs AI agents that run their daily operations, while their people stay in control.

## 2. The pain point

With the line set, the next question is what hurts. The webinar says the problem must feel real and well understood. So we name one buyer, one pain and one case.

| Part | Our answer |
|---|---|
| Who feels it | Sri Lankan service SMEs with 20 to 200 staff, a busy support inbox and online payments. The buyer is the founder or COO. |
| The pain | Every repeat case is decided by hand, from scratch. Refunds, billing, scheduling and care chats all work this way. Nobody records why a decision was made. So the know how leaves when a person leaves. |
| The proof case | A customer asks for a full refund on an LKR 85,000 annual plan, 41 days after buying. The policy says 30 days. Someone guesses, nobody records why, and next month the same case starts from zero. |
| Why it hurts more now | The AI tools people try have no approvals and no audit trail. Under the new data law, that becomes a legal risk. |

## 3. The metric that proves success

A pain needs a number that shows it is shrinking. The webinar calls this the metric that matters: the one you watch every month to know you are moving forward. Ours comes straight from the pain. If cases are decided by hand with no record, success is cases closed by the Brain with a full record.

| Role | Metric | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Headline** | **Governed auto resolution rate.** The share of routine cases the Brain closes on its own, where each case passed a policy check and left an audit record. | The Brain really does the work. It should rise each month, because solved cases become skills every night. |
| Support | Median time to resolve a case, against the customer's own baseline | Speed the customer can feel |
| Support | Share of risky actions that waited for approval and have a full audit trail. Target: 100%. | Governance, which is our edge and our answer to the data law |
| Business | Pilots converted to paid, then monthly churn under 4% | Customers keep paying |

We should not claim a result we have not measured. Instead, we measure it in two places. First, we run it on Finebase Labs, our own company and tenant number one. That gives a real number for the traction slide today. Then, in each free pilot, we report week one against week four. A line like "In week one, people handled every refund case by hand. By week four, the Brain closed X% itself, and every case has a record" is the kind of proof the webinar asks for.

## 4. The solution

Once the pain is clear, the solution slide shows how we remove it. The webinar warns against mixing topics here, so this slide shows the solution only.

Every job runs as a loop. A sensor notices what happened, like the refund email. Next, a policy decides what is allowed before anything acts. Then the agent does the work through the company's own systems. After that, a quality gate checks the result, and anything risky waits for the right person. Finally, each night, the cases people solved become reusable skills. As a result, the next refund like this one goes straight through.

## 5. Why now

A good solution still needs a reason to buy this year. We have three.

| Reason | Fact |
|---|---|
| The law | Sri Lanka's data protection rules start on 1 January 2027 (Gazette No. 2498/16). That is eleven weeks away. |
| The technology | AI agents have moved from chatting to acting. The market grows 46% a year (MarketsandMarkets). |
| The cost | Running an agent now costs cents per task, not a salary. |

## 6. The market

Next, the investor wants to know how big this can get. The webinar is strict here. It says to build the market bottom up, starting with SOM. It also says that "we only need 1% of the market" tells an investor you are chasing wild geese.

| Level | Number | How we got it |
|---|---|---|
| TAM | USD 52.6B | Global AI agents market in 2030 (MarketsandMarkets) |
| SAM | USD 62M | 10,405 mid size firms in Sri Lanka × USD 6,000 a year |
| SOM | USD 720K | 120 customers by end of year three × USD 6,000. Built from 2, 2.5 and 6 new customers a month. |

**Fix needed.** Our current script ends the market slide with "about 1% of these firms". That phrase is exactly the red flag the webinar names. Our math is already bottom up, so we should drop the percentage and end on the 120 customers.

## 7. Business model and unit economics

Here the pitch turns from product to business. The webinar says to state who pays, how much and how often, with no price ranges. It also asks for three unit economics numbers: LTV to CAC, payback period and gross margin.

| Question | Our answer |
|---|---|
| Who pays | The SME, as one company subscription |
| How much | Three fixed tiers: USD 299, 699 and 1,499 a month. The expected mix averages USD 500. |
| Why USD 299 | One support hire in Colombo costs about LKR 120,000 a month and covers one shift. Our agents work all day. |
| How often | Monthly |
| CAC | USD 1,800: free pilot about 900, founder time about 600, events about 300 |
| Gross margin | 75%. Model, cloud and storage cost about USD 125 of each USD 500. |
| Payback | About 5 months (1,800 ÷ 375) |
| LTV | USD 9,375 (375 a month × 25 months) |
| LTV to CAC | 5 to 1. The webinar says 3 to 1 or better is healthy. |

## 8. Traction

Numbers on paper need proof from the ground. The webinar says that before revenue, traction means letters of intent, live conversations and pilots. It also says investors care about this slide more than the product slides.

| What we have | What we still need |
|---|---|
| A working product: 35 screens across three user tiers | A real count of SMEs we have spoken to |
| A 15 stage governed pipeline, from first email to audit log | 5 signed letters of intent by end of January |
| 27 real use cases recorded on the live app | 3 paid pilots by end of January |
| Finebase Labs runs on it as tenant number one | A first governed auto resolution rate from our own tenant |

This is our weakest slide today. However, it is also the one we can fix fastest, because the metric from section 3 can be measured on our own company this week.

## 9. Go to market

Traction leads into growth. The webinar wants a specific plan, not "we will go viral". It asks who we target, where we reach them, how they buy and what it costs.

| Stage | Plan |
|---|---|
| Ideal customer | Service SMEs with 20 to 200 staff, a busy support inbox and online payments |
| Year one | The founder sells. Reach 300 SMEs from our network and SLASSCOM members. Run 80 demos and 40 free one month pilots. Convert half, so 20 paying customers. |
| Pilot cost | About USD 900 each |
| Year two | Accounting and audit firms resell us as their data law answer, for a 20% partner fee |
| Year three | Hire two salespeople. Start pilots in the Maldives and Bangladesh. |

**Fix needed.** The market slide says 2 new customers a month in year one, which is 24. The go to market slide says 20. The webinar says numbers must match every time, so pick one and use it everywhere.

## 10. Competition

A plan also has to survive rivals. The webinar warns never to say "we have no competition". Instead, name the two things a buyer needs, then place every player honestly.

Our two buyer needs are: agents that really do the work under real governance, and a price an SME can pay.

| Player | Does the work | Governed | SME price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff and spreadsheets | Yes, by hand | No | Yes |
| Zapier rules | Fixed rules only | No | Yes |

| Agentforce, Sierra | Yes | Yes | No |
| **Company Brain** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes** |

## 15. Delivery and questions

A strong deck can still fail on the day. So the webinar ends with delivery.

First, finish one minute under your slot. Use the 5 minute script for a 6 minute slot. Next, spend the most time on business model, traction, go to market and team. Problem, solution, market and ask can move faster. After that, prepare for questions. Feed the deck into an AI and ask for 30 investor questions, then practise a short answer for each. If you do not know an answer, say so, share a related fact, and promise the exact figure by a date. Finally, pitch it blind to friends and mentors before any investor sees it. Ask them what was clear and what was not. Then check one thing: could a judge explain our pitch in a room where we are not present? If yes, the deck is ready.
