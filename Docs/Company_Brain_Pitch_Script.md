# Company Brain pitch script

Companion to `Docs/Company_Brain_Pitch_Deck.pptx` (13 slides). The same words sit in each slide's speaker notes. ## Full script (5 minutes)

### 1. Cover (10s)

Hi, I'm Pamuditha from Finebase Labs. This is Company Brain: AI agents that run a company's daily operations, while its people stay in control.

### 2. Problem (25s)

Small and mid-size companies in Sri Lanka run their operations by hand. Care chats, refunds, billing, scheduling: one case at a time. The know-how sits in people's heads and inboxes, so when someone leaves, it leaves too. Take one real case. A customer asks for a full refund on an 85,000 rupee annual plan, 41 days after buying. The policy says 30 days. Today someone guesses, nobody records why, and next month the same case starts from zero. And the AI tools people try today have no approvals and no audit trail. Under the new data law, that is a real risk.

### 3. Solution (30s)

Company Brain fixes this. Every job runs as a loop. A sensor notices what happened, like that refund email. A policy decides what is allowed, before anything acts. The agent does the work through the company's own systems. A quality gate checks the result, and anything risky waits for the right person. On screen, a change that touches money waits for two leaders, with a replay of its effect on 318 past runs. And every night, the cases people solved become reusable skills. So the next refund like this one goes straight through.

### 4. Why now (15s)

Why now? Sri Lanka's data protection rules start on 1 January 2027, eleven weeks from today. AI agents have moved from chatting to acting, a market growing 46% a year. And running an agent now costs cents, not a salary.

### 5. Market (20s)

We sized the market bottom-up. We start with Sri Lanka's 10,405 mid-size firms. At 6,000 dollars a year each, that is a 62 million dollar serviceable market. Our three-year target is 120 customers: about 2 new customers a month in year one, 2 and a half in year two, and 6 in year three. That is 720 thousand dollars of annual revenue, about 1% of these firms.

### 6. Business model (35s)

We sell a monthly subscription per company, in three fixed tiers: 299, 699 and 1,499 dollars. We set 299 because one support hire in Colombo costs about 120,000 rupees a month and covers one shift, while our agents work all day. Our expected mix gives 500 dollars a month on average. Unit economics: winning a customer costs us about 1,800 dollars, mostly the free pilot and founder time. Gross margin is 75%, so we earn that back in 5 months. Over a 25-month customer life, each customer brings 9,375 dollars of gross profit. That is a 5 to 1 return on what we spend to win them.

### 7. Traction (35s)

This is not just an idea on slides. The product works today. We have 35 working screens across three tiers, for leaders, stewards and operators, and a 15-stage governed pipeline with typed contracts from the first email to the audit log. We recorded 27 real use cases on the live app. And we run our own company, Finebase Labs, on it as tenant number one. Next: by the end of January, 5 signed letters of intent and 3 paid pilots. So far we have spoken to [number] SMEs.

### 8. Go to market (35s)

Our first customers are Sri Lankan service SMEs with 20 to 200 staff, a busy support inbox and online payments. The buyer is the founder or COO. In year one, I do the selling myself. We contact 300 SMEs from our network and SLASSCOM members, run 80 demos and 40 free one-month pilots, and convert half, so 20 paying customers. Each pilot costs us about 900 dollars. In year two, accounting and audit firms resell us as their PDPA answer, for a 20% partner fee. In year three, we hire two salespeople and start pilots in the Maldives and Bangladesh.

### 9. Competition (20s)

We do have competition. Companies use staff and spreadsheets, Zapier rules, or support bots like Intercom Fin. Tools like Glean find answers but don't act. Agentforce and Sierra act and govern, but at enterprise prices. A buyer needs agents that really do the work, under real governance, priced for an SME, with Sri Lankan personal data masked. That is the corner we own.

### 10. Team (30s)

I built ChainSight, live dashboards with separate views for a District Secretariat, Divisional Secretariats and field officers. Company Brain uses the same tier idea, only now the field workers are agents. I designed and built the console and the pipeline. [Co-founder: name, role and one relevant line.] We are adding two advisors, each with one job: a data protection lawyer, and an SME sales leader.

### 11. Financials (20s)

Revenue grows from 60 thousand dollars in 2027 to 510 thousand in 2029. Costs grow slower, so we break even in year three, at 120 customers and 13 people. Net burn stays around 8 thousand dollars a month in the first two years, and we plan for churn under 4% a month.

### 12. The ask (20s)

We are raising 250 thousand dollars, pre-seed, for 24 months of runway. 45% goes to product, 30% to sales and pilots, 15% to AI and cloud, and 10% to legal, PDPA and a buffer. By month 24, it buys 50 paying customers and 300 thousand dollars of annual recurring revenue. Those are the numbers we need for our seed round.

### 13. Close (10s)

Every company deserves a brain it can trust. Thank you. My details are on the screen, and I'm happy to take questions.

## Likely questions and short answers

The webinar's advice: have a short answer ready for each, say it with confidence, and if you don't know, say so and promise a date.

| # | Question | Short answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Why would an SME trust an AI agent with refunds or money? | It can't act alone. Every action passes policy and access checks first, and anything that touches money waits for a named person. Two people approve the biggest changes. |
| 2 | What stops OpenAI or Microsoft from doing this? | They sell general tools. We sell a governed operating layer tuned for Sri Lankan SMEs: PDPA, LKR, local names and IDs masked, local onboarding. Big players don't run pilots for a 40-person company in Colombo. |
| 3 | How is this different from Zapier? | Zapier follows fixed rules and has no judgment or approvals. Our agents read the case, follow the policy, and stop for a person when risk is high. |
| 4 | You have no paying customers. Why should we believe the numbers? | The product works today and we run our own company on it. The ask funds exactly the proof: 5 LOIs and 3 paid pilots by January, 50 customers by month 24. |
| 5 | How did you get USD 1,800 CAC? | One free pilot (~900), founder sales time (~600), events (~300). It is a target; the pilots in the next 90 days will test it. |
| 6 | Why 4% monthly churn? | It is a cautious SME SaaS assumption. Our agents get better every night, which should push it down. We plan to beat 3% by year three. |
| 7 | What does a customer need to connect? | Their support mail (Google Workspace), payments (Stripe or PayHere) and calendar. More connectors switch on loop by loop. |
| 8 | Where is the data hosted? Is it PDPA compliant? | Each tenant is isolated, personal data is masked before any model sees it, and every access is logged. Hosting location under PDPA is an open question we are settling with legal advice. |
| 9 | What does it cost you to run one customer? | About USD 125 a month in model, cloud and storage cost, which gives the 75% margin. |
| 10 | What if the AI makes a mistake? | Every run is traced step by step. Risky actions wait for approval, actions use idempotency keys so they never run twice, and a failed tool call is retried, then rolled back. |
| 11 | Why start in Sri Lanka? | We know the market, the PDPA deadline creates urgency, and it is cheap to learn here before South Asia. |
| 12 | Who is your first paying customer likely to be? | A 20 to 200 person service company with a busy support inbox, sold founder to founder from our network. |
| 13 | Why will partners resell you? | Accounting and audit firms need a PDPA answer for their clients. We give them one plus a 20% first-year fee. |
| 14 | What happens after the 250K? | At 50 customers and 300K ARR we raise a seed round to build the sales team and enter South Asia. |
| 15 | What is your biggest risk? | Sales speed, not technology. That is why the first 90 days are about LOIs and paid pilots. |
| 16 | Why can't a customer just use ChatGPT? | ChatGPT answers. It doesn't act on company systems, follow company policy, ask for approval or keep an audit trail. |
| 17 | How long does onboarding take? | Target: the first loop live inside the one-month pilot. |
| 18 | Is the team full time? | Answer honestly for your team. |
| 19 | What is your moat? | The skills, policies and verified facts each customer builds up inside the brain. The longer they use it, the better it runs their company, and the harder it is to leave. |
| 20 | Why is pricing per company, not per agent? | SMEs want one clear bill. Tiers grow with the number of loops they switch on. |

If you are asked something you can't answer: "I don't have that exact number right now. Here is what I do know: [related fact]. I'll send you the exact figure by [date]."

## 7-day prep plan

| Day | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Fill in the blanks above. Read every number in "The numbers you must defend" until you can explain it without notes |
| 2 | Rehearse the 5-minute script aloud with a timer. Cut words until you finish at 4:45 |
| 3 | Pitch it blind to two friends or family. Ask what was clear and what was not |
| 4 | Fix the unclear parts. Rehearse the 3-minute version |
| 5 | Pitch to an advisor or mentor. Practise the 20 questions out loud |
| 6 | Run the live demo once (Brain overview, an approval, the fleet) as a backup if a judge asks to see it |
| 7 | Two full run-throughs. Export a PDF copy of the deck in case the venue has no PowerPoint |
