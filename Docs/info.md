## Part B. How the judges want a pitch (L7 webinar "Pitch like a pro")

The organisers asked every team to apply this webinar. These are its rules, in short.

### B1. Core rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| 2.5 minute reader | An investor looks at a deck for about two and a half minutes. Every slide must answer one question with clear numbers. |
| Headline is the message | Each slide title is a take-home sentence, not a label like "Business model". |
| One objective per slide | Solution slide shows only the solution. Don't mix topics. |
| Readable and minimal | No large paragraphs, no small text. Clean charts. Simple fonts and colours. Must work as a PDF and on a phone. |
| Start from one line | Write the one-line pitch first. Then one sentence per slide. Each sentence becomes that slide's headline. |
| Short deck | About 12 slides. A 40 to 50 slide deck will not be read. |
| Time | Finish at least one minute under your slot. |
| Where to spend time | Most time on business model, traction, go to market and team. Move faster on problem, solution, market and ask. |
| The room test | Could a judge explain your pitch in a room where you are not present? If not, the deck is not ready. |

### B2. The 12 slides the webinar asks for

| # | Slide | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover and one-liner | What is this, in one sentence? |
| 2 | Problem | Is the pain real and well understood? Who feels it? |
| 3 | Solution | How does it remove the pain? |
| 4 | Why now | Why this year? |
| 5 | Market (TAM, SAM, SOM) | How big can it get? Built bottom up, starting with SOM. |
| 6 | Business model and unit economics | Who pays, how much, how often? LTV to CAC, payback, gross margin. |
| 7 | Traction | What proof exists? Revenue, users, pilots, letters of intent, conversations. |
| 8 | Go to market | Ideal customer, where to reach them, how they buy, what it costs. |
| 9 | Competition | The two things a buyer needs, and where each player honestly stands. |
| 10 | Team | Key people and relevant experience only. Advisors with a real job. |
| 11 | Financials | Revenue and cost forecast. Burn rate, churn rate, headcount. |
| 12 | The ask | How much, for how long (about 2 years), and the milestone it buys for the next round. |
| + | Close | Vision line, plus name, email and phone on screen. |

### B3. Red flags the webinar names

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| "We have no competition" | Shows you did not research. A judge will find three rivals on their phone. |
| "We only need 1% of the market" | Shows you don't know your customers. Build bottom up instead. |
| Price ranges ("5 to 10 dollars") | Shows uncertainty. Give one price with a reason. |
| Growth claims with no basis | Every number must be backed by facts. |
| Numbers that change between slides | Numbers must be consistent everywhere. |
| "We'll go viral" or "Google ads" as the plan | Investors want a founder who does the ground work. |
| Advisors added for their name only | Each advisor needs a specific job. |

### B4. Q&A advice

| Advice | Detail |
|---|---|
| Prepare | Feed the deck to an AI, get 20 to 30 likely questions, practise a short answer for each. |
| Confidence over perfection | How clearly you answer matters more than the answer itself. |
| If you don't know | Say so, share a related fact, and promise the exact figure by a date. Never bluff. |
| Test it | Pitch it blind to friends and mentors first. Ask what was clear and what was not. |

### C2. The one line

> Company Brain gives Sri Lankan SMEs AI agents that run their daily operations, while their people stay in control.

Vision line for the close:

> Every company deserves a brain it can trust.

### C3. The problem

| Part | Detail |
|---|---|
| Who feels it | Sri Lankan service SMEs with 20 to 200 staff, a busy support inbox and online payments. The buyer is the founder or COO. |
| The pain | Every repeat case is decided by hand, from scratch: refunds, billing, scheduling, care chats. Nobody records why a decision was made, so the know-how leaves when a person leaves. |
| Proof case | A customer asks for a full refund on an LKR 85,000 annual plan, 41 days after buying. The policy says 30 days. Someone guesses, nobody records why, and next month the same case starts from zero. |
| Why it hurts more now | The AI tools people try have no approvals and no audit trail. Under the new data protection law, that becomes a legal risk. |
| Context | SMEs are about 52% of Sri Lanka's GDP and about 45% of employment. |

### C4. The solution

Every job runs as a loop of five steps.

| Step | What happens | In the refund case |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Sensor | Notices what happened | The refund email arrives |
| 2 Policy | Decides what is allowed, before anything acts | Refund is outside the 30-day window and above the auto limit |
| 3 Tool | The agent does the work through the company's own systems | Drafts the refund through the payment gateway |
| 4 Quality gate | Checks the result. Anything risky waits for the right person. | Waits for the Head of Customer Success to approve |
| 5 Learning | Each night, cases people solved become reusable skills | Next late refund goes straight to approval, no escalation |

Demo moment from the script: a change that touches money waits for two leaders, with a replay of its effect on 318 past runs.

### C5. Approval follows risk (criticality)

| Level | Who approves |
|---|---|
| C0 | Automatic |
| C1 | Supervisor agent (L2) |
| C2 | Human steward (L2) |
| C3 | The named owner (DRI) at leadership level (L1) |
| C4 | Two different leaders, plus a cooling period |

A change that touches money, personal data, public content, legal or tax, or more than one area moves up one level.

### C6. Three user tiers

| Tier | Who | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Command | Founders, directors, owners | See the business at a glance, approve high-risk changes, provide evidence, protect the legal entity |
| L2 Governance | Stewards and supervisor agents | Write and test skills, policies, security, templates and knowledge before anything goes live |
| L3 Operations floor | Worker agents and operators | Agents do the work. Operators watch live, inspect runs, take over and "Skillify" |

Higher tiers can look down, never up.

### C8. Enterprise benefits

| Benefit | What it means |
|---|---|
| Work finishes without people, safely | Agents resolve routine work inside their skills |
| Approval weight follows risk | Routine changes flow. Risky ones stop for the right person. Top level needs two leaders. |
| Nothing goes live blind | Sandbox tests, replay on real past runs and a plain-language diff before approval |
| Ground truth you can trust | Every fact has evidence, a valid time, a recorded time and a verifier. Conflicts are caught. |
| Security at the moment of action | Agents can't exceed their own rights or the rights of the person they act for. Every decision is logged. |
| Never do one-off work | Skillify turns how a person handled a case into a tested, versioned skill |
| Protects the legal entity | Deadlines watched 21 days ahead, evidence requested early, scoped and revocable data grants |
| Full accountability | One named owner (DRI) per object, and a read-only, hash-chained audit trail |
| Scales | Hundreds of agents and tens of thousands of runs, versioned and rollbackable like git |
| Privacy | Personal data (including Sri Lankan names and IDs) is masked before any AI model sees it |

### C9. How it works under the hood

A raw signal goes in. A governed outcome comes out. On the way it is masked, routed, reasoned over, checked, approved if needed, executed and recorded. Every finished run feeds learning.

| Lane | Turns | Key steps |
|---|---|---|
| A Signal to action | A signal into a governed action | Store raw, mask personal data, dedupe, route to a skill and agent, retrieve context, Claude proposes, access check, policy and quality gates, approval if needed, execute once, record, audit |
| B Document to ground truth | A document into verified facts | Parse, chunk and embed, extract facts, librarian finds conflicts, change request, merge without overwriting |
| C Learning | Finished runs into better skills | Nightly review or Skillify, classify failures, draft a fix in a sandbox, run tests and replay, approve, release |
| D Readiness | Deadlines into early evidence requests | Hourly check, find missing evidence, ask an agent or the owner, escalate at 21, 14, 7 and 2 days |

Three rules hold everywhere: nothing acts before governance, approval weight follows risk, and nothing is ever overwritten (everything is versioned).

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Pipeline | 15 stages, each one process with one job, joined by 15 typed contracts |
| Speed targets | Routine chat reply under 10 s (p95). Access check under 10 ms. Retrieval under 400 ms. |
| Safety | Fails closed if the access service is down. Approvals never auto-approve on expiry. Actions use idempotency keys so they never run twice. |

### C10. Tech stack

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Console | Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind, TanStack Query |
| API and services | FastAPI, Pydantic v2 (Python) |
| Event backbone | Kafka (Redpanda locally, Amazon MSK in production) |
| Workflows | Temporal (long approval waits, timers, retries) |
| Database | PostgreSQL 17 with per-tenant row security, pgvector for search |
| Cache | Valkey |
| Files | S3 |
| Access rules | OPA (Rego) |
| AI models | Claude Haiku (volume), Sonnet (agents), Opus (rare hard calls) |
| Embeddings | Voyage AI |
| Parsing and privacy | Docling, Presidio with Sri Lankan recognisers |

| Hosting | AWS (ECS, RDS, S3, KMS, Secrets Manager) |

Why Kafka and Postgres both: Postgres holds the truth. Kafka carries events between stages, in order, replayable, so one slow stage never blocks the rest.

From the product's own design rule (calm, spacious, few words) and the webinar.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
